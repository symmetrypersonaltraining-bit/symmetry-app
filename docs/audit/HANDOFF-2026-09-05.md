## ⚠️ FOUND ON THE WAY OUT — A REAL BUG, NOT BUILT

Dustin asked the right question: *"if i do move the session in my schedule, the
app should be set to automatically move that supervised workout in the app to
the matching day according to my calendar. does this effect that?"*

**It does, and it already did before tonight.** Not caused by any change made
here — but now that clients are explicitly encouraged to move supervised
workouts, it will start happening far more often.

`sync_supervised_workouts_to_appointments()` runs on cron three times a day and
pulls a supervised workout onto its appointment's date. Its guard is:

```sql
and (sw.moved_from_date is null or sw.moved_by = 'calendar_sync')
```

and the trigger `stamp_workout_mover()` stamps `moved_by = 'manual'` for every
move that is not the sync itself — the board, the day sheet, the logger, and the
AI acting for a client.

So **the first time anyone moves a supervised workout by hand, that workout is
permanently opted out of calendar sync.** Dustin then moves the appointment in
Google Calendar and the workout does not follow, silently, forever. Live count
today: **15 of the 259 future supervised sessions are already in that state.**

The guard is asking the wrong question. It asks *"has a person ever moved this?"*
when it should ask *"who moved it most recently?"* Both moves are legitimate: a
client moving it because he is away is real, and him moving the appointment is
real and later. The later one should win.

**Proposed fix** (needs his go-ahead — a cron touching 259 rows, and per the
standing rules a `bak_*` backup first):

1. Add `moved_at timestamptz` to `scheduled_workouts`, stamped by
   `stamp_workout_mover()` on every date change. A dedicated column, not
   `updated_at`, because `updated_at` is bumped by logging and position writes
   too and would make an untouched workout look recently moved.
2. Widen the guard to let the calendar win when it is newer:
   `... or a.updated_at > sw.moved_at`.
   `gcal_sync_appointments()` sets `updated_at = NOW()` only when a field
   actually changed, so it is a true "when he last changed this booking" clock.
3. Backfill `moved_at` from `updated_at` for existing manual moves, and decide
   what to do about the 15 already stranded.

Result: a client's move sticks, until he moves the appointment — then the
calendar takes it back, which is what he expects and what he asked about.

## Standing rules that bit tonight — worth knowing before they bite again

- **The ship bridge.** Cloud sessions cannot push. Commit → `git bundle create`
  into `/tmp` then copy to outputs → `device_commit_files` the bundle THEN
  `SHIP-NOW` → poll `SHIP-RESULT.txt`. Two things caught me: a **full** bundle
  is 24 MB and the bridge caps files at 20 MB, so always bundle
  `origin/main..main main`; and another session pushed mid-flight, so on
  "not a fast-forward" rebase onto `origin/main` and resend rather than forcing.
- **Trainer identity test.** Do not put his name inside a prompt string —
  `COACH_FIRST_NAME` or a code comment. It failed the suite twice.
- **Tests re-anchor, they do not weaken.** `moveWorkoutAnywhere` asserted a
  literal sentence I deliberately narrowed. The premise was still right, so the
  fix was to re-anchor it AND assert the new exception, not to delete the check.
