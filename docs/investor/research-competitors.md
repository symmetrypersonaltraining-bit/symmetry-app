# Personal-trainer software: competitor, market and pricing research

Prepared 11 September 2026 for a business plan for a new entrant positioned on corrective exercise / movement assessment for post-rehab and over-40 clients.

## 0. Method and source caveat (read first)

- Research was done with web search plus page fetches. **Every direct page fetch was blocked by the sandbox's network egress proxy** – this included all ten vendor pricing pages, Capterra, G2, Reddit, BLS, Wikipedia, Crunchbase, BusinessWire, PR Newswire, Garmin's newsroom, IDEA and HFA. So no figure below was read off a vendor's own pricing page by me.
- What I could use: search-engine result snippets and summaries of third-party 2026 pricing round-ups (quickcoach.fit, coachway.io, coachbox.app, assistantcoach.fit, promealplan.com, frontdeskreview.com, aitoolsbakery.com, fitbudd.com's competitor posts), review aggregators (Capterra/G2/GetApp/SoftwareAdvice), press-release mirrors (Yahoo Finance, MarketScreener, GlobeNewswire) and industry bodies (HFA, IDEA, BLS) as they appeared in search results.
- Many of those round-ups are written by competitors (quickcoach, coachway, assistantcoach, fitbudd, promealplan, coachbox are all rival products). They are usually accurate on list price but have an incentive to emphasise add-ons and fees. Where two independent round-ups agree I mark the figure "cross-checked"; where they disagree I show both.
- Dates: most third-party pages only show "2026" in the title. Where a page carried a more specific date in the snippet (e.g. "re-confirmed August 2026", "as of March 2026") I give it. Otherwise the date is recorded as "2026 (no day shown)".
- **Before any number here goes into the plan, re-check it against the vendor's own pricing page from a normal browser.** The vendor URLs are listed so that is a 10-minute job.
- Reddit r/personaltraining could not be reached and search did not return thread-level results, so section 5 uses Capterra/G2/Trustpilot/App-Store complaints instead. This is stated explicitly rather than guessed.

### Pricing notes

1. **Price at the 50-client point (the mid-career solo trainer)** – the most useful single anchor: PT Distinction $89.90 (everything in), Kahunas $69–99, CoachRx $79, Hevy Coach $90 (workouts only), Trainerize ≈$135 monthly + $10 payments + $45 nutrition ≈ $190, TrueCoach $164.98, Everfit $95 + $9 + $39 ≈ $143, My PT Hub $105, TrainHeroic $74.99, FitBudd $79 + 30×$2 = $139. A PT Distinction comparison page puts the "all features, 50 clients" figures at PTD $89.90 / Trainerize $175 / TrueCoach $164 / Everfit $197+ (ptdistinction.com comparison, 2026, vendor-authored).
2. **Cliffs**: TrueCoach jumps from $69.98 to $164.98 at the 21st client (a recurring Capterra complaint); TrainHeroic jumps by 50-athlete bands; CoachRx from $79 to $149 at the 51st client.
3. **Payment-fee spectrum**: 0% platform fee (PT Distinction, Kahunas, FitBudd, My PT Hub, Trainerize – Stripe fees only) → 2% (CoachRx) → 5% (TrueCoach, if the 2026 reports are right).
4. **Add-on stacking** is the dominant pricing complaint and is concentrated in Trainerize and Everfit; PT Distinction, Kahunas, CoachRx and FitBudd market "no add-ons" against them.
5. **Trial length** clusters at 14 days (TrueCoach, Kahunas, CoachRx) or 30 days (Trainerize, My PT Hub, FitBudd, Hevy Coach, PT Distinction's "1 month"); Everfit uses a permanent free tier instead.
6. **Annual discounts** cluster at 15–25% (Trainerize 15–20%, Everfit 16%, My PT Hub ~10–14%, Kahunas 25%, CoachRx 15%, TrueCoach ~12–17%).

### 4.2 Demand-side (HFA)

- 81M Americans were members of a fitness facility in 2025 (26.1% penetration, ages 6+). 65+ was the fastest-growing cohort, +8.6% YoY. (HFA 2026 US Health & Fitness Consumer Report, healthandfitness.org; athleticbusiness.com coverage.) Quality: high (industry body, annual consumer panel).
- 23% of members used a personal trainer in 2024; average 21 PT sessions per user, down from 28 in 2019; women's PT participation +15.9% to 7.3M. (HFA 2025 Consumer Report: Expanded Insights.) Quality: high.
- **Adults 55+ are the least likely to use a trainer – under 10% do** (HFA "Personal Training Usage Continues to Grow"). Quality: high. This is the single most relevant demand statistic for the over-40 / post-rehab positioning: the fastest-growing membership cohort is the least penetrated by PT.

### 4.3 Share of trainers already using coaching software

No independent survey with a clean "% of trainers who pay for coaching software" figure was found; say so in the plan. Proxies:

- Vendor-claimed coach counts (global, include free and dormant accounts): Trainerize 400k+, Everfit 200–320k, My PT Hub 130k, TrueCoach 20k, Kahunas 14k. Summed they exceed the entire US profession, confirming they are cumulative sign-ups, not paying users. Quality: low.
- ABC Trainerize 2026 State of the PT Industry Report (survey of its own users, so biased toward software adopters): ~1 in 2 coaches now say hybrid is their primary delivery model; 64% use AI tools regularly (71% marketing, 61% nutrition, 52% programming); 4 in 5 say finding new clients is harder or has stalled; ~4 in 10 report rising demand for nutrition/habit/mental-wellness support. (healthclubmanagement.co.uk press release, 2026; trainerize.com/blog.) Quality: medium for direction, low for population shares.
- A trainer-preference survey summarised on mypthub.net/llcbuddy (2025–26) has Trainerize named "best" by ~31%, Mindbody 15.4%, PT Distinction 12.8%. Quality: low (sample and sponsor unclear).

## 5. Inputs for pricing a corrective-exercise / post-rehab / over-40 entrant

### 5.1 Where incumbents are weak (feature gaps relevant to this niche)

- **Movement assessment is essentially unserved.** Of the ten, only PT Distinction has built-in movement screens (video-recorded tests, posture, custom assessment templates). Everfit, My PT Hub and Kahunas offer generic forms; Trainerize, TrueCoach, Hevy Coach, TrainHeroic and FitBudd have nothing. None of the ten turns an assessment result into a corrective-exercise prescription automatically. (Sources in section 2.)
- **The adjacent clinical tools do not cover the trainer's business.** Physitrack (£17.99/user/mo standard; ~$10.99/practitioner for clinics ≤10 users; Telehealth add-on £/€/$11.99) and MedBridge (Essentials $329/seat/yr for 100 HEP episodes, Elite $369/seat/yr unlimited) are physio home-exercise-program tools: strong exercise libraries, no billing, no client-programme periodisation, no coaching messaging built for trainers. (capterra.com/p/10040456/Physitrack/, support.physitrack.com, pricingnow.com/question/medbridge-pricing, 2026.) There is white space between "PT-clinic HEP tool" and "general coaching app".
- **Over-40/older clients are under-served on the demand side**: 65+ is the fastest-growing gym cohort (+8.6%) while <10% of 55+ members use a trainer (HFA, 2025–26).
- **Nutrition is a weakness for TrueCoach, Kahunas, Hevy Coach and TrainHeroic**, and a paid add-on for Trainerize and Everfit – but nutrition is a secondary need for this niche; do not over-invest to win on it.
- **Scheduling with native Google Calendar sync** is only genuinely native in Trainerize and FitBudd; everyone else relies on Zapier or nothing. For a hybrid in-person/online trainer it is a cheap differentiator.

### 5.3 Churn and retention – what is public

- **No vendor in this set publishes its own subscriber churn.** Xplor, ABC Fitness and Jonas are private or do not break the products out; EverCommerce's 10-K only said fitness was <4% of revenue (FY2023).
- Kahunas/Stripe case study: Kahunas "decreased customer churn by 9%" after moving to Stripe Billing (stripe.com/customers/kahunas, undated). Relative, not absolute.
- Coachway (a competitor) publishes first-party **client** retention on its platform: 94% at month 1, 82% at month 3, 65% at month 6, 45% at month 12, n = 5,666 Nordic clients (coachway.io/articles/online-fitness-coaching-statistics/, 2026). This is trainer-to-client retention, not trainer-to-software retention, but it drives seat counts and therefore the trainer's willingness to pay.
- Category benchmarks (compilations, low quality): "fitness & wellness SaaS" ~5.5% monthly churn vs 3.5% B2B SaaS average (focus-digital.co 2025 citing Recurly; retentioncheck.com 2026); SMB self-serve SaaS 3–7% monthly, 43% of SMB losses in the first 90 days (genesysgrowth.com, optif.ai, 2026). Plan for **4–6% monthly logo churn** and front-load onboarding.
- "Standard" personal-trainer client retention 75–80%, dipping to 70% seasonally (blog.everfit.io, unsourced) – low quality.

### 5.4 Pricing implications

1. **Anchor**: a solo trainer with 20–50 clients currently pays $60–165/month; the no-add-on competitors (PT Distinction $89.90, Kahunas $69–99, CoachRx $79) set the "fair" all-in reference at **~$70–90/month at 50 clients**. A specialist tool with an assessment engine can justify the top of that band or slightly above (e.g. $79–119) provided there is no payment fee and no 20-client cliff – the two things reviewers punish most.
2. **Do not charge a platform % on client payments** – 0% is the norm (PT Distinction, Kahunas, FitBudd, My PT Hub, Trainerize) and TrueCoach's 5% and CoachRx's 2% are actively resented.
3. **Bundle, don't add-on**: reviewers reward PT Distinction/Kahunas/CoachRx for "everything included" and penalise Trainerize/Everfit for stacking. If a branded app is offered, the market price is $95–169 one-time or $99–149/month (My PT Hub, Trainerize, Kahunas Ultimate).
4. **Trial and annual**: 14 or 30 days with no card, and 15–25% off annual, are table stakes.
5. **Client-count tiers** are universal; avoid a >2× jump between adjacent tiers (TrueCoach's 20→21 cliff is the most-cited complaint in the category).
6. **Value framing for the niche**: the over-40 / post-rehab trainer sells fewer, longer, higher-priced engagements (HFA: sessions per user falling, older cohort growing, <10% penetration) – price on retained-client value and assessment/re-assessment cadence rather than on session volume.
7. **Adjacent price ceilings**: clinical HEP tools cost $11–18/user/month (Physitrack) or ~$27–31/seat/month (MedBridge annual) – a trainer-facing product that also includes coaching, billing and messaging at $79–119 is defensible against them.
