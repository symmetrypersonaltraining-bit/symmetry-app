3. What the market says

Where competitors are weak: only PT Distinction has any movement assessment, and none of the ten turns an assessment into a corrective programme. Trainers' top complaints are hidden add-ons, payment fees, price cliffs at 20 or 50 clients, client-app crashes that lose a workout mid-session, and support decay at the two acquired leaders (Trainerize, TrueCoach).

Market size, honestly: BLS counts 322,930 employed trainers and instructors in the US (May 2025); independents push the total to roughly 450,000 to 600,000. Published "market size" reports range from $0.8B to $13.8B and are not usable. A bottom-up estimate of US trainer-software revenue is roughly $80M to $260M a year. The incumbents took 10+ years and full teams to reach $5M to $25M; none raised more than about $4.5M.

How founder-led launches in this category actually went: with no marketing budget, the base case is under $1,000 a month at month 12 (about 70% of indie software never passes that line). The closest analogue to you, a gym co-owner selling a programming tool to peers, plateaued at $7,000 to $8,000 a month. TrueCoach's founders reached about $100,000 a month in five years by putting savings into paid ads. Acquisition spend is what separates those outcomes, which is why the launch budget matters more than the build budget.

5. What the launch brings in

A cohort model: each month, marketing spend divided by the cost to win one paying trainer gives new trainers; a monthly churn rate removes some; the first two months run at 60% and 85% because the funnel, site and waitlist are built before launch (Option C). Individuals are modelled at 5, 15 or 30 new a month with 10% monthly churn, scaled to marketing spend. Trainers at the $115 average, individuals at $49. Marketing stays at $20,000 a month after launch; the model was also run with marketing cut to $10,000 after month 6, and every scenario got worse, because acquisition is the growth engine. Keep the spend unless the funnel is not working.

The signal to read in August is cost per paying trainer: under $900 and holding, keep going; over $1,200, stop paid spend.

Why these numbers and not bigger ones. The base case needs roughly 190 trial sign-ups a month at 10 to 15% conversion from a $20,000 budget. No company in this category has published growth like that from a standing start; TrueCoach and Trainerize took years. The model assumes paid channels work from month 3, which is the single biggest risk in the plan and the reason the review exists.

6. Terms and what they mean in dollars

Your terms: a committed $274,000 loan facility to the app LLC at 7% a year, drawn month by month only as the business needs it, so interest accrues only on money actually in use (about $238,000 on the base path, $146,000 on the optimistic), with payments starting the month the app first covers its own running costs and then amortising over five years. Lauren becomes a 10% owner of the LLC, written into the operating agreement, with 10% of profit distributions for as long as she holds it and the right to sell her interest back to you later. Profit is drawn by either of you only once the business account holds four months of expenses in retained earnings. The LLC is the only borrower; your training income is separate and untouched. Option B: if Lauren does not want to fund the developer, the $80,000 developer line is borrowed elsewhere.
